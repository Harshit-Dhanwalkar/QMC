QMC Playground — double-well tunnelling

Classically a particle in one well of a double well stays there. Quantum mechanically the two lowest levels form a doublet — an even and an odd state a tiny energy Δ apart — and a particle placed in the left well is the difference of the two, so it tunnels to the right and back with period 2π/Δ. Raise the barrier and Δ collapses exponentially (compare with the WKB estimate in the bottom plot). The same physics flips the nitrogen of the ammonia molecule through its hydrogen plane, the transition behind the first maser. Eigenstates of H = −½∂² + B(1−x²/a²)² by the QMC library (double_well.c), ℏ = m = 1.

The wells sit at x = ±a with small-oscillation frequency ω = √(8B)/a; WKB gives Δ ≈ (ω/π) exp(−∫√(2(V−E0))dx) across the barrier. A packet is projected onto the lowest eight eigenstates (the captured weight is reported) and evolved exactly, ψ(t) = Σk ck e−iEkt ψk. Narrow packets in the harmonic ground state of a well are close to the two-state form cos²(Δt/2); wider or off-centre packets add faster oscillations from the higher doublet. Time is shown as a fraction of the tunnelling period so a deep barrier stays watchable. Source: physics/double_well.c, wrapper wasm/qmc_doublewell.c; the solver, the WKB scaling and the tunnelling law are verified in tests/test_double_well.c.
