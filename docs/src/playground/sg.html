QMC Playground - Stern-Gerlach analyzers

A Stern-Gerlach magnet splits a beam of spin-½ atoms into an “up” and a “down” beam along its axis. Block one output and you have measured the spin: send that beam through a magnet turned by 90°, block one output again, and a third magnet along the original axis finds half of those atoms “down” again — z+ → x+ → z+ passes ¼ of the beam, though z+ → z+ passes all of it and z+ → z− none. Let both beams through and bring them back together and the state is restored (Feynman’s “Humpty Dumpty” magnet) — but only as far as the two packets still overlap. Chain up to three analyzers, tilt them, mix the source, and compare the screen pattern with the classical smear. Bloch-vector bookkeeping by the QMC library (stern_gerlach.c).

The beam is a Bloch vector r with intensity I. An analyzer along n has outputs I(1 ± r·n)/2; blocking one gives r = ±n (a projective measurement). Passing both through a reversed gradient maps r → (r·n)n + c(r − (r·n)n), where c = exp(−d²/8σ²) is the overlap of the two Gaussian packets: for d ≫ σ the “which beam” record is in the atom’s position and the spin is decohered, exactly as if it had been measured. The classical curve is a magnetic moment with random orientation, z = (d/2)cos α, convolved with the packet width: a smear rather than two spots. Axes are tilted in the x-z plane (negative angles point toward −x). Source: physics/stern_gerlach.c, wrapper wasm/qmc_sg.c; the chains, the Zeno staircase, recombination and the packet overlap are verified in tests/test_stern_gerlach.c.
