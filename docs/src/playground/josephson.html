QMC Playground — Josephson junction

The phase difference φ across a Josephson junction obeys the equation of a damped, driven pendulum, φ″ + φ′/Q + sin φ = i, which is a ball rolling in a tilted washboard potential. Below the critical current (i < 1) the ball sits in a well and the voltage is zero; above it the ball runs and the voltage is its average speed. Drag the bias current on the I–V plot to tilt the washboard; raise Q to see hysteresis, add an ac drive to lock the phase and carve flat Shapiro steps. Integrated by the QMC library (josephson.c, RK4).

Time is in units of 1/ωp and voltage is the mean phase velocity ⟨φ′⟩. With Q → 0 the curve is V = Q√(i²−1); a heavily underdamped junction switches at i = 1 but retraps only near 4/(πQ) on the way down, giving the hysteresis loop. With an ac drive of frequency Ω the running phase can lock to the drive and the mean voltage stays pinned at nΩ across a range of dc current — the principle of the Josephson voltage standard. The I–V sweeps carry the state from one current to the next, which is what makes the loop. Source: physics/josephson.c, wrapper wasm/qmc_josephson.c; the supercurrent branch, overdamped formula, convergence, hysteresis and Shapiro plateau are verified in tests/test_josephson.c.
