QMC Playground — Quantum Zeno effect

A resonant drive (Rabi frequency Ω = 1) flips a qubit from |0⟩ to |1⟩ in time π. Interrupt it N times with a measurement of “still in |0⟩?” and each short interval barely moves it, so the survival probability cos2N(π/2N) tends to 1: watching it freezes it. A continuous measurement is modelled as dephasing at rate Γ; the decay is fastest at Γ = 2Ω and then slows as Ω²/Γ. Both are computed live by the QMC library (zeno_survival_pulsed, zeno_survival_continuous) and checked against Monte-Carlo trajectories.

Pulsed: survival cos2N(ΩT/2N) → exp(−(ΩT)²/4N). Continuous: dz/dt = −Ωy, dy/dt = Ωz − Γy with z = P0 − P1, slowest rate Γ/2 up to Γ = 2Ω and (Γ − √(Γ²−4Ω²))/2 beyond. The blue dots are the fraction of simulated qubits that passed every measurement so far. Real Zeno experiments (trapped ions, cold atoms, superconducting qubits) see exactly this suppression, and its opposite, the anti-Zeno speed-up, in other regimes this two-level model does not cover.
