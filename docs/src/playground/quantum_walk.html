Flip a quantum coin, then step left or right. Because the coin stays in superposition, the two paths interfere: the walker spreads in proportion to time t and piles up near the edges of the light cone, while the classical random walk (same coin, no interference) only reaches √t. Every step is a coin-and-shift update in the QMC library (qwalk_step), running in your browser.

Up moves right, down moves left; each step first applies the coin [[cos θ, sin θ], [sin θ, −cos θ]] to every site. Source: physics/quantum_walk.c, wrapper wasm/qmc_qwalk.c. The variance obeys ⟨x²⟩/t² → 1−sin θ (checked in tests/test_quantum_walk.c); the classical curve is the exact binomial distribution.
