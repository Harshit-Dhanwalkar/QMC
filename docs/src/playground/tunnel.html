QMC Playground — resonant tunnelling

A single thin barrier almost always reflects a slow particle. Put two barriers around a well and at a few special energies the particle goes straight through with probability exactly 1: the waves bouncing between the barriers interfere constructively, a Fabry–Perot cavity for matter. Drag the energy (click and drag in the stage or on the transmission plot) and watch |ψ|² pile up in the well on resonance. Transfer matrices computed by the QMC library (double_barrier.c), units ℏ²/2m = 1 so E = k².

In each layer the pair (ψ, ψ′) is carried by the unimodular matrix [[cos qd, sin qd/q], [−q sin qd, cos qd]] (cosh/sinh under a barrier), and T = 4/[(M11+M22)² + (kM12−M21/k)²]. Resonances sit just below the levels (nπ/w)² of the well (the wavefunction leaks into the barriers, making the well effectively wider); their width shrinks exponentially with barrier thickness and the dwell time (∫|ψ|²dx over the structure, divided by the incident flux 2k) grows as the inverse width. With unequal barriers the peak transmission falls below 1 — the right barrier × control makes that visible. Source: physics/double_barrier.c, wrapper wasm/qmc_tunnel.c; closed forms, probability conservation and the resonance conditions are verified in tests/test_double_barrier.c.
